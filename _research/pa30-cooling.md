Pa 30 is the remnant of the historical supernova of 1181, and its ejecta are drawn out into long, narrow, radial filaments, giving it a dandelion-like appearance. Ordinary remnants do not look like this.

In [Duffell, Polin & Mandal (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13641), we showed that efficient cooling of the ejecta can sculpt this morphology.

[Pikus et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ae57ac) turned this into a general framework. 3D hydrodynamic simulations, run with Sprout, use a single dimensionless parameter, β, to set how quickly thermal energy is radiated away, without assuming a particular cooling mechanism. For β of about 400 or more, cooling acts on a timescale near 1/400 of the Sedov time and the ejecta form long, narrow filaments: Rayleigh–Taylor fingers that cooling protects from being overturned by the Kelvin–Helmholtz instability. These filaments move at 95–100% of their free-expansion speed.

Matching this regime to Pa 30 implies an explosion energy near 3.5 × 10<sup>47</sup> erg and a cooling luminosity of roughly 10<sup>36</sup> erg s<sup>−1</sup>.
